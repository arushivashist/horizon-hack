Why it stays flat

  • 1Every page starts a fresh conversation. The model sees the system prompt, its memory, and the new page. Nothing else from earlier pages comes along.
  • 2Memory is capped: at most 12 lessons and 3 noise notes of 400 characters each, about 1,500 tokens even when full.
  • 3Inside a page it keeps everything it has done, at most 15 queries. When the page closes, that conversation is thrown away and only checked lessons carry over.
  • 4So the most it ever reads is one page's work plus a small, fixed memory. The baseline instead carries everything since its last summary, so it climbs with every page until it's squashed, then climbs again.

    What we did

    1. A month of on-call at a fake store. Northstar Commerce, built by our teammate: 39 pages, of which 35 are false alarms and 4 are real incidents, including a repeat on day 22 and a look-alike on day 24 whose old runbook advice is wrong. Agents investigate with SQL, see only records visible at page time, and never see the answer key.
    2. Two agents, one difference. Both run Claude Opus 5 with the same tools and a 15-query budget per page. History + summaries keeps one conversation for the whole month and squashes it into its own summary at 30,000 tokens. Safe memory keeps full history inside a page, then carries only short lessons forward.
    3. Memory that can't be poisoned or bloated. Every write must cite pages the agent closed; nothing is written mid-investigation; instruction-like text, links and credentials are refused; slots and sizes are capped; every attempt lands in an append-only log that can replay memory as of any moment.
    4. Where the sponsors fit. Nimble captures vendor status pages (the day-9 proof is a Nimble snapshot of PayRail), Tinybird holds the telemetry, memory log and agent events, and a small Liquid model is the planned first responder for the nightly false alarms.
